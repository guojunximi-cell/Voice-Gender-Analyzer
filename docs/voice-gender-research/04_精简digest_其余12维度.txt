## Transmasculine (FTM) voice & testosterone effects
OVERVIEW: Gender-affirming testosterone therapy (T) is the single most powerful tool for masculinizing the transmasculine voice, and unlike estrogen (which does NOT raise pitch in trans women), exogenous T reliably and irreversibly lowers fundamental frequency (F0/pitch) by thickening the vocal folds. This is the defining asymmetry between FTM and MTF voice transition: trans men get pitch "for free" from hormones, whereas trans women must train it manually. The mechanism is vocal-fold mass/thickness increase (the folds get heavier and more flaccid, dropping F0); critically, in post-pubertal adults the vocal folds thicken but do NOT meaningfully lengthen, and the laryngeal cartilage framework is already ossified, so the masculinization is incomplete relative to cis male puberty where folds both lengthen and thicken. Typical outcome: female-baseline F0 of roughly 190-210 Hz drops to a male-range ~12
KEY FINDINGS (claims):
  - [well] Testosterone lowers trans men's speaking F0 into the cis-male range, with mean F0 ~125 Hz at 12 months and wide individual variation (89-170 Hz).
  - [well] The pitch drop is largest in the first 2-6 months and plateaus by ~12 months; first changes can appear within 2 weeks.
  - [well] Mean total pitch decrease after 12 months of T is roughly 6-9 semitones.
  - [well] Masculinization is INCOMPLETE for ~15-25%: about 21% fail to reach the cis-male F0 range after one year and ~16% are dissatisfied with their voice.
  - [well] Testosterone thickens the vocal folds but does NOT lengthen them or the vocal tract, producing an F0-resonance mismatch.
  - [mode] F0 lowering does NOT correlate with circulating androgen levels — dose/level does not predict how much the voice drops.
  - [mode] ~24% of trans men experience voice symptoms (instability, fatigue, hoarseness) during transition and require/seek voice therapy.
  - [mode] Laryngoscopy of trans men on T shows high rates of hyperfunctional/compensatory laryngeal behaviors.
  - [mode] Voice masculinization TRAINING adds masculinity beyond what T delivers — clinically ~35 Hz of additional pitch lowering plus resolution of fatigue/instability.
  - [well] Voice satisfaction tracks lower F0, and voice is among the highest-rated outcomes of T overall.
QUANT:
  - Trans men mean speaking F0 after 12 months on T = ~125 Hz (range ~89-170 Hz, large interindividual variation), n=50; baseline female-typical ~190-210 Hz
  - Cis reference F0 vs trans men on T = trans men 116.8 Hz (±16.9); cis men 110.6 Hz (±16.6); cis women 192.5 Hz (±25.7) — adults age 20-40
  - Typical within-participant F0 reduction on T = ~60-70 Hz (≈80 Hz from female baseline), stable after ~3-4 months
  - Mean F0 decrease in semitones at 12 months = ~6.4 semitones (mean); case study 183→134 Hz = -5.4 ST; some cohorts ~9 ST over first 9 months
  - Timeline of largest pitch drop = steepest months 1-6 (e.g. -1.8 ST months 1-2, -1.9 ST months 3-4); first changes within ~2 weeks; plateau ~12 months
  - Fraction failing to reach cis-male F0 range after 1 year of T = ~21% (meta-analysis of 19 studies)
  - Voice dissatisfaction after T = ~16% dissatisfied; ~21% report incomplete voice-gender congruence/voice problems; ~25% sometimes perceived as female on phone
  - Trans men reporting voice symptoms (instability/fatigue) needing voice therapy = 24% (n=50)
  - Estimated vocal tract length (incomplete resonance masculinization) = trans men ~15.7 ± 0.8 cm (intermediate: longer than cis women d=1.0, shorter than cis men d=1.4); 23% outside cis male range
  - Additional pitch lowering from voice therapy beyond T = ~35 Hz; plus resolution of fatigue and pitch instability
  - Laryngoscopic findings in trans men on T = ventricular fold constriction ~50%, incomplete glottal closure ~50%, vocal fold erythema ~42%, excessive secretions ~42%
  - Vocal fold thickening onset = within first ~4 months; folds thicken but do NOT lengthen in post-pubertal adults
  - Upper vocal range restriction = ~32% of trans masculine participants show restricted upper range; max F0 e.g. 941→746 Hz
  - Patient satisfaction with voice change from T = 77% rated voice masculinization extremely satisfied (1-2 scale) — highest-rated masculinizing trait

Concrete implications: (1) Pass/no-pass must NOT rely on F0 alone. ## Transfeminine (MTF) voice feminization
OVERVIEW: Voice feminization for trans women is a multidimensional behavioral and (optionally) surgical undertaking, not a pitch-only problem. The contemporary clinical consensus, codified in protocols such as Davies & Goldberg's "Clinical Aspects of Transgender Speech Feminization" (Intl J Transgenderism, 2006/2008) and Mills & Stoneham's "The Voice Book for Trans and Non-Binary People" (Charing Cross GIC, 2017), is that femininity perception is driven by FOUR critical acoustic-physiologic elements: (1) fundamental frequency (F0/pitch), (2) vocal-tract resonance (vocal-tract length/larynx height), (3) formant tuning (especially F1-F4), and (4) phonatory pattern / vocal weight (vocal-fold mass & adduction). Secondary targets include intonation/melody, articulation precision, and pragmatics/word choice. The single most robust empirical finding is that pitch alone is NEITHER necessary nor sufficient
KEY FINDINGS (claims):
  - [well] Perceived gender has a fundamental-frequency crossover around 155-165 Hz, but pitch alone is insufficient to be heard as female.
  - [well] Resonance/formant frequencies are a primary (arguably the dominant) cue to vocal femininity, independent of pitch.
  - [well] Voice feminization therapy targets four elements: F0, resonance (vocal-tract length/larynx height), formant tuning, and phonatory pattern (vocal weight).
  - [mode] Intonation pattern (melody) significantly predicts perceived gender: more upward inflection and wider semitone range read as feminine.
  - [mode] Behavioral voice therapy reliably raises speaking F0 by ~25-39 Hz and improves perceived femininity, but gains are variable and can regress.
  - [mode] Phonosurgery is an adjunct that raises pitch but not resonance/formants/intonation; combined surgery+therapy beats surgery alone.
  - [mode] The best acoustic predictors of perceived femininity/gender attribution are F0, average vowel formant frequencies, and sound pressure level.
  - [mode] Therapy dosage is typically a few months of weekly sessions, but total session time does not significantly predict F0 gain.
QUANT:
  - Adult cisgender female speaking fundamental frequency (mean) = ~196-224 Hz, mean ~205 Hz; typical range ~145-275 Hz (adults)
  - Adult cisgender male speaking fundamental frequency (mean) = ~107-132 Hz, mean ~116 Hz; typical range ~80-165 Hz (adults)
  - Perceptual F0 crossover for trans women heard as female vs male = ~155-165 Hz (Gelfer & Schofield ~160 Hz crossover); 'ambiguous pitch range' ~145-165 Hz; ~180 Hz for reliably feminine (Gorham-Rowan & Morris 2000)
  - Trans women with F0 in cis-female range still judged male = >70% of MTF speakers (despite F0 matching biological-female speakers)
  - Membranous vocal-fold length, male vs female = Male 14.97 ± 2.01 mm; Female 11.17 ± 0.68 mm (~25% longer in males)
  - Vocal-fold thickness/width, male vs female = Male 6.07 ± 1.11 mm; Female 5.03 ± 1.10 mm
  - Voice feminization therapy F0 gain (behavioral) = +~27 Hz vowel /a/; +~39 Hz reading; +~25 Hz spontaneous speech (single-study range 15-55 Hz)
  - Phonosurgery F0 gain (pooled) = +~71.7 Hz vowel /a/; +~41 Hz reading; +~39 Hz spontaneous (single-study high 110 Hz anterior glottic web)
  - Combined therapy + surgery vs surgery alone (F0 gain) = ~107 Hz combined vs ~76 Hz surgery alone
  - Wendler glottoplasty mean F0 gain at 12 months = ~50.25 Hz (F0 on /a/)
  - VFSRAC (vocal-fold shortening + retrodisplacement of anterior commissure) F0 change = Pre 144.1 Hz → Post 207.4 Hz at 6 months (+73.6 Hz), MTF cohort n=362
  - Cricothyroid approximation (CTA) durability/satisfaction (older series) = ~50% of patients satisfied; ~31% 'passable' voice
  - Laser-assisted voice adjustment (LAVA) F0 gain = ~26 Hz (insufficient to reach female pitch range)
  - Therapy dosage = 5-84 total sessions, typically 1-3x/week, ~60 min/session; therapy time NOT a significant predictor of F0 gain (meta-regression p=0.625)
  - Intonation goal prevalence and perceptual effect = Intonation a stated goal for 68% of MTF clients; perceived-female speakers used more upward inflections and greater utterance semitone range
  - Practitioner feminine target speaking-pitch range = ~165-255 Hz (cis-female average); consistently ≥165 Hz tends to read feminine

IMPLICATIONS: CLASSIFIER DESIGN (pass / uncertain / no-pass): Do NOT build a pitch-only detector — the literature is unambiguous that F0 alone misclassifies the majority of trans women. The model must jointly extract and weight at least: (1) speaking F0 (median + range), (2) vowel formants F1-F4 / estimated vocal-tract length, (3) a resonance/'brightness' proxy (formant dispersion, spectral tilt), (4) vocal weight / phonation proxies (open quotient, breathiness/HNR, spectral slope), and (5) intonation features (semitone range, % rising contours, % level/monotone). Train labels on listener-perceived gender, not just acoustic bins. THRESHOLDS: Encode an explicit gender-ambiguous F0 band (~145-165 Hz) as 'uncertain' on pitch, with ~180 Hz as 'comfortably feminine' for F0 — but make the final verdict a multi-feature decision so a user with F0 ≈ 200 Hz but male-typical formants is correctly flagged 'no-pass / uncertain' with the reason 'resonance/formants unchanged.' Use cis-female norms (mean ~205 Hz, formants raised ~15-20%) and cis-male norms as the two reference distributions for normed scoring and for an interpretable femininity score.

## NN/ML gender & voice-attribute classification + uncertainty estimation from audio
OVERVIEW: Speaker gender classification from audio is one of the most mature and "easy" paralinguistic tasks: binary male/female accuracy on clean, read-speech benchmarks (Common Voice, VoxCeleb, TIMIT, RAVDESS) routinely sits in the 92-99%+ range across virtually every model family, because the dominant cues — speaking fundamental frequency (F0) and vocal-tract-length-driven formant spacing — are strongly bimodal in the cisgender adult population. The methodological lineage runs from (1) hand-crafted acoustic features (MFCCs, F0/pitch, formants F1-F4, spectral and prosodic descriptors, and the standardized 88-dim eGeMAPS / openSMILE sets) fed to classical classifiers (SVM, GMM, logistic regression, random forest, XGBoost); to (2) end-to-end deep models on spectrograms/MFCCs (CNN, CRNN, LSTM/BiLSTM, attention CNNs); to (3) speaker-embedding back-ends (i-vector, x-vector TDNN, ECAPA-TDNN) where a s
KEY FINDINGS (claims):
  - [well] Binary speaker gender classification is a near-solved task on clean read-speech benchmarks, with most model families reaching 92-99%+ accuracy.
  - [well] Speaker embeddings (x-vector, ECAPA-TDNN) carry gender almost perfectly: a simple classifier on the embedding reaches ~97-99%.
  - [well] Self-supervised transformer encoders (wav2vec 2.0 / HuBERT / WavLM) are the current SOTA for age/gender and are more robust across conditions than CNN baselines; gender info is present and stable across nearly all transformer layers.
  - [well] Fundamental frequency (F0) is the single most important acoustic determinant of perceived voice gender, with vocal-tract-length / formant cues (F3, F4, average formant, VTL) as the dominant second factor.
  - [mode] Voice gender can be modeled as a CONTINUOUS / spectrum quantity (perceived femininity-masculinity), and machine scores correlate meaningfully with human perception.
  - [mode] Binary/cisgender-trained gender classifiers misgender and poorly serve trans and non-binary speakers; the field is being pushed toward continuous, perception-aligned, identity-respecting modeling.
  - [well] Deep classifiers are systematically overconfident; temperature scaling is the strongest cheap post-hoc calibration baseline, enabling a trustworthy confidence number behind pass/uncertain/no-pass.
  - [well] Selective classification (reject option) lets the model abstain on low-confidence inputs via a threshold on max-softmax-probability or predictive entropy, giving a principled accuracy-coverage tradeoff — i.e. the 'uncertain' verdict.
  - [well] Conformal prediction yields distribution-free prediction SETS with guaranteed coverage; the ambiguous set {female, male} is itself the natural 'uncertain' output.
  - [mode] Evidential deep learning and MC-dropout/ensembles provide single-pass or sampling-based uncertainty usable for audio reject options.
  - [well] There is a well-recognized ~145-165 Hz 'ambiguous/androgynous' speaking-F0 zone separating typically-male-perceived from typically-female-perceived voices, which is the natural anchor for the 'uncertain' band and for coaching targets.
QUANT:
  - CNN on MFCC features, binary gender accuracy (custom test set) = 99.22% (1662 of 1675 test speakers correct)
  - 1-D CNN stacking MFCC + Mel-spectrogram + Chroma, binary gender accuracy = 97.8%
  - Random Forest on ECAPA-TDNN embeddings, gender accuracy on VCTK test split = 99.1% (NeMo embeddings); 97.0% (SpeechBrain embeddings)
  - X-vector (with data augmentation) gender identification accuracy = ~99%
  - Fine-tuned wav2vec2 gender classification accuracy/F1 (benchmark probing) = 95.4%
  - audeering wav2vec2-large-robust age/gender model — minimum gender accuracy across child/female/male; UAR gains vs classical = min 91.1% gender accuracy; +44% gender UAR, +99% age UAR; age MAE 7.1-10.8 yrs
  - audeering model training data composition = VoxCeleb2 30,300; aGender 29,553; TIMIT 1,570; Common Voice 1,729 samples; base = Wav2Vec2-Large-Robust; pooled last-layer hidden states -> 1024-unit FC heads
  - Attention-CNN over spectrograms, gender accuracy on Common Voice = ~96%
  - BiLSTM (2 layers) on Common Voice, gender accuracy/precision (validation) = 0.93 accuracy / 0.94 precision
  - Machine 'voice gender score' (Extreme Random Forest) correlation with human perceptual ratings = r = 0.63 (female speakers); r = 0.77 (male speakers); optimal sample ~7 s
  - eGeMAPS standardized acoustic feature-set size = 88 features (F0 stats, jitter, shimmer, HNR, formants F1-F3, spectral, MFCC, loudness)
  - Cisgender adult speaking F0 ranges and androgynous zone = cis female SF0 ≈ 165-255 Hz; cis male ≈ 85-155 Hz; androgynous/ambiguous ≈ 145-165 Hz; perceived-female typically requires sustained ≥ ~165 Hz; cis female formants ~20% higher than cis male
  - Largest prospective trans-voice training study — strongest acoustic predictors of 'female-sounding' listener ratings = F0, average formant frequency, and Leq (SPL); n = 74 trans women + 40 cis reference speakers, 79 naive listeners
  - Selective classification benchmark — softmax-response thresholding error at partial coverage = 2% top-5 ImageNet error at 60% coverage (guaranteed risk via SR threshold)

## Open-source projects, models & apps for voice gender analysis + trans voice training
OVERVIEW: The tooling landscape splits into three reusable tiers for a "does my voice pass / how do I train it" product. (1) Acoustic-feature engines that compute the gender-relevant primitives — fundamental frequency (F0/pitch), formants (F1–F4, the core of vocal-tract "resonance"/"size"), HNR, jitter/shimmer, and spectral tilt. The dominant open-source stack here is Praat (the reference phonetics engine) exposed to Python via Parselmouth, plus librosa for general DSP, openSMILE for standardized feature sets (GeMAPS/eGeMAPS/ComParE), and dedicated pitch trackers (CREPE/torchcrepe, librosa.pyin/pYIN). These give you transparent, explainable numbers you can map directly to gender-perception thresholds (e.g. F0 and formants), which matters because users and clinicians trust interpretable metrics over black-box "score" outputs. (2) Neural speech toolkits and pretrained gender/age classifiers — Speech
KEY FINDINGS (claims):
  - [well] Parselmouth is the de-facto standard for getting Praat-quality F0 and formant measurements into Python, and is what most research/clinical trans-voice analysis builds on.
  - [well] A small family of wav2vec2 models on HuggingFace are the most reusable pretrained gender classifiers, but they are binary/3-class and trained on cis speech.
  - [well] The trans community and clinicians treat perceived voice gender as a two-axis problem — pitch (F0) AND resonance (formants/vocal-tract length) — not a single scalar.
  - [well] CREPE/torchcrepe is the strongest open neural pitch tracker; classical pYIN (in librosa) is the standard transparent baseline.
  - [well] There is a mature set of trans-specific training apps that define UX/pedagogy norms; most give real-time visual feedback and deliberately avoid a hard male/female verdict.
  - [well] openSMILE's GeMAPS/eGeMAPS standardized feature sets are the most citable, paralinguistics-validated feature vector for a classifier, but licensing limits commercial use.
  - [well] SpeechBrain, pyannote.audio and ESPnet are the permissive neural toolkits for speaker embeddings/diarization that you can build a custom (trans-aware) gender/pass head on top of.
  - [mode] Numerous beginner-grade 'gender by voice' GitHub repos exist but mostly classify with shallow features on small/cis datasets and overstate accuracy.
  - [well] VoiceLab is a ready-made, citable, Parselmouth-based GUI/library that batch-measures exactly the gender-relevant acoustics, useful as a reference for which metrics to compute.
  - [mode] Pretrained cis-binary gender models are a poor fit for the trans pass/uncertain/no-pass framing and must be recalibrated, not used off-the-shelf.

  - audeering age+gender model output format = age in [0,1] mapped to 0-100 years; gender = P(child), P(female), P(male) (3-class)

(2) Pass-detector — do NOT take argmax of an off-the-shelf binary gender model. ## Acoustic measurement & real-time biofeedback tooling
OVERVIEW: Real-time gender-voice biofeedback rests on two acoustic primitives: fundamental frequency (F0/pitch) and the lower vocal-tract resonances (formants F1-F2-F3, a proxy for "resonance"/vocal-tract length). Pitch is the easier, more robust signal; resonance is harder to track reliably and harder to render legibly to a lay user. For pitch, the practical algorithm families are (a) time-domain autocorrelation/difference-function methods — YIN and its probabilistic extension pYIN (used by Praat-style trackers and librosa); (b) spectral template matching — SWIPE/SWIPE'; and (c) neural methods — CREPE (CNN on raw 1024-sample frames), SPICE (self-supervised, constant-Q), PESTO, and newer low-latency variants (PENN, SwiftF0). CREPE achieves >90% raw-pitch accuracy at a 10-cent tolerance and is more noise-robust than pYIN/SWIPE below ~10 dB SNR, but the full model is heavy (22.2M params, ~30 ms look
KEY FINDINGS (claims):
  - [well] CREPE (deep CNN pitch tracker) reaches >90% raw pitch accuracy within 10 cents and near-100% on clean studio data, and is more noise-robust than pYIN/SWIPE below ~10 dB SNR
  - [well] Algorithm choice is a real accuracy tradeoff: YIN/pYIN are robust time-domain standards, SWIPE is strong on clean signals but degrades in noise, CREPE wins at low SNR but is heavy
  - [well] SPICE is a deployable on-device/browser pitch model: self-supervised, ships as TensorFlow.js and TensorFlow Lite, runs on 16 kHz mono audio
  - [well] Real-time formant/resonance tracking is error-prone and sex-biased; LPC-Burg formants are only a rough approximation and need sex-specific settings
  - [well] Classic pitch pitfalls — octave errors, subharmonic capture, and voicing misclassification — disproportionately hit low/creaky voices relevant to trans-masc users
  - [mode] Real-time F2 visual biofeedback can shift resonance and raise perceived femininity — the most direct evidence for gender-voice biofeedback — but on small samples with unproven generalization
  - [mode] TruVox demonstrates a feasible privacy-preserving, validated real-time gender-voice pitch-trainer architecture
  - [mode] Visual biofeedback improves in-session target acquisition but transfer/generalization is inconsistent and evidence quality is low
  - [well] Motor-learning theory says constant high-frequency feedback harms long-term retention; faded/reduced knowledge-of-results is better for durable skill
  - [well] Browser real-time pitch is feasible within a tight latency budget using AudioWorklet + WASM
  - [well] Auditory feedback delay becomes perceptually disruptive around 50-200 ms, bounding tolerable end-to-end latency for any 'live' feedback
  - [mode] Spectrograms and raw formant displays are powerful but hard for lay users; visual+audio feedback beats audio-only
  - [well] Voice Range Profile (phonetogram) is the standard visualization of vocal capability and a clinically endorsed assessment
QUANT:
  - CREPE raw pitch accuracy within 10-cent tolerance = >90% (MDB-stem-synth); ~100% (RWC clean data)
  - CREPE model size (full vs tiny) = full = 22.2M parameters; tiny = 487k parameters; 360 pitch bins at 20 cents/bin over 6 octaves; 10 ms default step; ~30 ms lookahead
  - TruVox pitch-engine validation vs Praat = <5 Hz error on pure tones; mean difference 15.4 Hz (SD 10.7) on 16 transfeminine speakers' Rainbow Passage; pitch range setting 50-600 Hz
  - TruVox usability and learning outcome (n=21) = System Usability Scale mean 79.8 (SD 12.8); 6 of 7 exercises significant error reduction (P<.05), improvements 1-8 Hz; age M=30.2 (SD 8.0)
  - Cisgender adult speaking F0 norms = Adult female ≈ 205 Hz (other sources 183-210 Hz, SD ~20 Hz); adult male ≈ 116-120 Hz (SD ~18-20 Hz)

IMPLICATIONS: Concrete guidance for building the NN/LLM pass-detector + voice-coach:

PITCH ENGINE (verdict + coaching): Run a robust F0 tracker with an explicit voicing/confidence gate and octave-continuity smoothing — pYIN's HMM voicing or CREPE/SPICE confidence head — because octave errors and subharmonic (creak) capture will otherwise corrupt the pass/no-pass decision, especially for trans-masc users at low pitch. For the cloud analyzer, full CREPE (MIT) is a strong accuracy reference; for the live browser coach, use SPICE (TF.js/TFLite, Apache-2.0) or a WASM YIN/MPM (pitchfinder/pitchy, MIT) inside an AudioWorklet. Validate any lightweight tracker against Praat/Parselmouth the way TruVox did (target ~<15 Hz mean agreement).

RESONANCE: Do not surface raw multi-formant overlays as the primary signal — LPC-Burg formants are sex-biased (male overestimation), unreliable on back vowels, and need per-speaker max-formant ceilings. Compute F1-F3 with Praat/Parselmouth server-side for scoring, but render resonance to users as a friendlier proxy (an F2/F3-driven "vowel-space dot" tracking a target, or resonance band-energy), echoing the validated F2-target paradigm that demonstrably raised femininity

## SLP pedagogy & evidence-based voice-training methodology
OVERVIEW: Gender-affirming and clinical voice training rest on a shared scientific backbone: the Principles of Motor Learning (PML), drawn from limb-motor and speech-motor research and codified for SLP by Maas et al. (2008, AJSLP). PML splits training conditions into (a) practice conditions — amount, distribution (massed vs distributed), schedule (blocked vs random), variability (constant vs variable), attentional focus (internal vs external), and target complexity; and (b) feedback conditions — type (knowledge of performance KP vs knowledge of results KR), frequency (100% vs faded), and timing (immediate vs delayed). The consistent meta-principle is a phase split: conditions that boost in-session ACQUISITION (massed, blocked, constant practice; internal focus; frequent immediate KP) differ from — and are often the opposite of — conditions that boost long-term RETENTION and TRANSFER/generalization
KEY FINDINGS (claims):
  - [well] Motor learning has a phase structure: conditions that maximize in-session acquisition (massed, blocked, constant practice; internal focus; frequent immediate KP feedback) are often opposite to those that maximize long-term retention and transfer (distributed, random, variable practice; external focus; faded/delayed KR feedback).
  - [well] External attentional focus (attend to the resulting SOUND or resonance sensation/effect, not to muscle/articulator placement) produces more automatic movement and better retention than internal focus.
  - [well] Semi-occluded vocal tract exercises (SOVT) — straw/tube phonation, lip trills, humming, /m v z/ — work by raising supraglottal inertive reactance, which feeds acoustic energy back to the vocal folds, lowers phonation threshold pressure, and yields easy, efficient phonation.
  - [well] Flow-resistant straw/tube (SOVT) therapy and Lessac-Madsen Resonant Voice Therapy (LMRVT) are roughly equally efficacious for reducing voice handicap in dysphonia; both beat no-treatment controls.
  - [mode] Resonant Voice Therapy (LMRVT) improves voice quality/efficiency in dysphonia, but its evidence base is only 'moderate' in strength and limited in volume.
  - [mode] For gender-affirming voice feminization, ~8–12 weekly 45–60 min sessions produce clinically meaningful, largely durable acoustic change; intensive schedules give comparable results to traditional weekly delivery.
  - [mode] Gender-affirming voice gains are partially durable but can regress without continued practice; some acoustic parameters hold at 3-month and 1-year follow-up while others do not.
  - [mode] The traditional exercise hierarchy (sustained vowel → syllable → word → phrase → sentence → reading → conversation → spontaneous) advances at ~80% accuracy per level, but rigid hierarchies that isolate skills out of context can IMPEDE generalization to conversation.
  - [mode] Distributed (spaced) practice generally favors retention/transfer over massed practice, but the advantage is not universal for complex motor skills — and in speech/voice, massed schedules can also work.
  - [mode] Vocal hygiene and systemic hydration have supporting evidence for improving/protecting voice quality; warm-ups produce measurable short-term acoustic improvement.
  - [mode] Adherence to home voice practice is the principal limiting factor for outcomes; non-adherence/dropout reaches ~74% in some reports, and app-based feedback roughly halves missed home-practice tasks.
  - [mode] Motor-learning concepts can be operationalized into observable, codable clinician/training behaviors and a structured 'prepractice' phase, enabling systematic (and potentially automatable) coaching.
  - [prel] Community/practitioner pedagogy frames voice gender as multiple semi-independent dimensions (pitch/F0, resonance/'size', vocal weight/fold mass, intonation, articulation) rather than pitch alone — and explicitly warns against pressed/strained phonation while training.
QUANT:
  - Feedback-fading schedule used in SLP motor-learning practice (acquisition → retention) = From 100% of trials (every attempt) faded to ~80%, 50%, 20%, then 0%; reduced/delayed feedback favors retention
  - Accuracy threshold to advance hierarchy level (traditional articulation/voice therapy) = ≥80% accuracy at a level before progressing to the next
  - Optimal straw diameter for beneficial SOVT back-pressure = ~3.5 mm diameter produced among the highest beneficial back pressure; RCT protocol used a 5 cm × 2.5 mm thin straw
  - SOVT home-practice dosage in RCT (flow-resistant tube arm) = 4 times/day, ~4 min each, 10 tokens per exercise, weekly 55–60 min clinic sessions, over 4 or 8 weeks
  - Typical gender-affirming voice feminization course length = ~8–12 weekly sessions of 45–60 min (some programs require ≥8 attended)
  - Example reading F0 change after feminization training = 146.2 → 203.6 Hz (12 sessions); 144 → 165 Hz (10 sessions); spontaneous 140.9 → 195.2 Hz
  - Cis-female reference mean speaking F0 (adult cohorts cited) = ≈199 ± 28 Hz (Australian speakers); ≈188 ± 20 Hz (Swedish speakers); 'gender-neutral/ambiguous' band ≈140–165 Hz
  - Intensive vs traditional gender-affirming schedule comparison = Traditional 1×45 min/wk × 12 wk (n=17) vs Intensive 3×45 min/wk × 4 wk (n=17); comparable outcomes
  - Warm-up durations tested for acoustic effect = 5–20 min warm-up; jitter/shimmer/NHR decrease, HNR/CPPS/alpha-ratio increase post-warm-up (short-lived)
  - Voice therapy non-adherence / dropout magnitude = Dropout/poor-attendance reported up to ~74.4%; app-based support cut missed home-practice tasks by ~50%
  - Hydration effect on voice acoustics = Dehydration worsens NHR, shimmer, jitter, frequency, s/z ratio; water ingestion improves shimmer, jitter, frequency, and maximum phonation time; caffeine not clearly harmful

  - [mode] Voice goals are diverse and often non-binary; feedback must be GOAL-RELATIVE to the user's own stated target, not an imposed cis-binary ideal

## Non-binary / androgynous / individualized voice targets (diversity of user goals)
OVERVIEW: Gender in voice is not a binary switch but a continuum, and a growing body of perceptual, acoustic, and clinical work now supports targets anywhere along (and off) a femininity–masculinity axis. The central empirical anchor is the "gender-ambiguous" fundamental-frequency (F0) zone: cis-male mean speaking F0 sits near 116 Hz (SD ~12) and cis-female near 205 Hz (SD ~24), and listener gender judgments become unstable roughly between 145 and 165 Hz (some authors give a wider neutral band of ~150–185 Hz; one synthesis study used ~143 Hz as a "neutral" anchor). Inside this zone, F0 alone stops determining perceived gender and secondary cues dominate — formants/vocal-tract length (resonance), source spectral tilt, articulation, and intonation. ## Voice Conversion (VC) for trans voice: RVC, so-vits-svc, DDSP-based & streaming low-latency VC — as feminization/masculinization aids, self-voice biofeedback ("hear my own voice shifted to target"), and target-exemplar coaching. Pitch/timbre disentanglement, perceptual passing of converted voices, on-device feasibility, and licenses.
OVERVIEW: Voice conversion (VC) — transforming a source speaker's speech to sound like a target while preserving linguistic content and prosody — has become a fast-moving, heavily community-adopted technology among trans people, yet it is almost entirely absent from clinical voice-training literature. The dominant open-source toolchain is built around content/speaker disentanglement: a self-supervised content encoder (HuBERT/ContentVec/WavLM) strips speaker identity to leave "what is said," an F0 (pitch) extractor (RMVPE, CREPE, FCPE, Harvest, DIO) captures prosody, and a generator/vocoder (VITS-style, NSF-HiFiGAN, BigVGAN, or a DDSP synthesizer) re-renders the audio in the target timbre. RVC (Retrieval-based Voice Conversion) is the most widely used in the trans community because it trains a usable any-to-one model from ~10 minutes of target audio and exposes an explicit semitone "Transpose" cont
KEY FINDINGS (claims):
  - [well] RVC (Retrieval-based Voice Conversion) is the most widely community-adopted VC for trans feminization/masculinization; it trains an any-to-one model from ~10 minutes of target audio and exposes an explicit semitone pitch control for cross-gender shifts.
  - [well] Standard SVC-family VC (RVC, so-vits-svc, DDSP-SVC) works by content/speaker disentanglement: a self-supervised content encoder removes speaker identity, an F0 extractor carries prosody, and a VITS/HiFiGAN/DDSP generator re-renders the target timbre.
  - [well] Koe AI's LLVC is the lowest-latency open-source VC: <20 ms algorithmic latency at 16 kHz and ~2.8× faster than real-time on a consumer CPU, any-to-one, MIT-licensed.
  - [well] Google StreamVC runs streaming VC fully on-device: 10.8 ms compute per 20 ms chunk on a single Pixel 7 CPU core, ~70.8 ms end-to-end, and exposes explicit soft-F0 and energy controls enabling deliberate pitch/loudness manipulation.
  - [well] Seed-VC provides zero-shot (no per-target training) real-time VC, cloning a target voice from 1–30 s of reference audio, with ~300 ms algorithm + ~100 ms device latency — but it is GPLv3 (copyleft).
  - [well] DDSP-based VC parameterizes audio into pitch, loudness, and a spectral/timbre envelope, naturally disentangling pitch from timbre — closer to what gender-affirming therapy manipulates (resonance vs. pitch) — and is far more CPU/real-time friendly than so-vits-svc.
  - [well] Real-time acoustic/resonance biofeedback (the non-VC analogue of the 'hear/see your voice vs. target' feature) has peer-reviewed evidence of helping trans women hit feminine formant/resonance targets.
  - [well] Gender-affirming voice TRAINING reliably changes acoustics and listener-perceived gender and improves vocal satisfaction — establishing that the *target* of any VC-coaching tool (the trainee's own unaided voice) is trainable.
  - [prel] There is essentially NO peer-reviewed controlled evidence that neural VC, used as a coaching/biofeedback aid, improves a trainee's own (unaided) voice, nor formal perceptual-passing studies of VC-converted output for trans speakers.
  - [mode] Latency thresholds matter: for a live 'pass-through' or real-time biofeedback feature, total mouth-to-ear latency should ideally sit well under ~100 ms to avoid disrupting the speaker; typical RVC GPU setups hit ~90–150 ms while on-device/CPU LLVC and StreamVC reach <20–70 ms.
QUANT:
  - RVC minimum target training data for a usable model = ~10 minutes of low-noise target audio (base model trained on ~50 h VCTK)
  - RVC cross-gender pitch shift (male→female) = Transpose = +12 semitones (one octave)
  - RVC real-time end-to-end latency = ~170 ms standard; ~90 ms with ASIO drivers (hardware-dependent); 50–150 ms typical reported
  - LLVC latency and real-time factor on CPU = <20 ms algorithmic latency @16 kHz; ~2.8× faster than real-time on a consumer CPU
  - StreamVC on-device compute and end-to-end latency = 10.8 ms compute per 20 ms chunk on one Pixel 7 CPU core; 70.8 ms end-to-end (60 ms architectural); 20 ms/320-sample chunks @16 kHz, 50 Hz inference
  - StreamVC quality metrics (LibriTTS) = DNSMOS 4.07; WER 6.22%; CER 2.17%; F0 Pearson r=0.842; speaker similarity 77.81%
  - Seed-VC real-time latency and zero-shot reference length = ~300 ms algorithm + ~100 ms device (~400–430 ms total); clones target from 1–30 s reference audio
  - so-vits-svc data requirement and sample rate = ~1–2 hours target audio for a decent model; resampled to 44.1 kHz mono; clips 5–15 s
  - DDSP-SVC training data and sample rate = ~1,000 clips (each ≥2 s) recommended; default 44.1 kHz; lower compute than so-vits-svc
  - Voice Femininity / listener-uncertainty study = Corpus 41 cis/trans speakers, 57 listeners; +5% 'I don't know' for trans voices; response time ~6.2 s (trans-female) vs 3.4–3.7 s (cis)
  - Gender-affirming voice training outcome (trans women) = Effects maintained 3 months; ~2/3 increased vocal satisfaction to a relevant degree

IMPLICATIONS: Concrete implications for the NN/LLM pass-detection + voice-coaching tool:

1) Add a VC-powered 'target preview / shifted self' feature, but keep it clearly separate from the pass detector. The most-requested community use is hearing oneself shifted toward a target. Two complementary modes: (a) zero-shot target-exemplar preview (Seed-VC-style) so a user picks/uploads a target-gender reference voice and hears their own speech rendered in it — great for goal-setting; and (b) explicit-control preview using DDSP/StreamVC-style soft-F0 + spectral-envelope so the user can independently move PITCH and RESONANCE sliders and hear the effect — which maps onto what voice therapy actually trains (resonance/formants, not just F0). Crucially, evaluate the user's OWN unaided voice with the pass detector, never the VC-converted audio, or the pass score becomes meaningless.

2) On-device feasibility is real. LLVC (MIT, <20 ms, 2.8× real-time on CPU) and StreamVC (70.8 ms on a Pixel-7 CPU core) prove a live mic-to-ear loop is achievable without a GPU and even on a phone — viable for a browser/WASM or mobile biofeedback loop.
